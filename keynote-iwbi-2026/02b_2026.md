# Section 2b — Era II → III: 2026, the Present-Day Test (and the Brink of Era III)

## Slide 2 — 2026: two prospective studies show AI screening works.

And here is where it stands in 2026: there are now two large prospective studies showing AI works in a real screening program. **MASAI**, the Swedish randomized trial of about **105,000** women, reported its interval-cancer result — **non-inferior, ratio 0.88** — at a **sensitivity of 80.5% versus 73.8%** for the same specificity, and it cut reading workload by about **44%**. **PRAIM**, a real-world German deployment of **463,000** women across **12 sites and 119 radiologists**, found **17.6% more cancers detected** with AI support and **no increase in recall** (**6.7 vs 5.7 CDR per 1,000**). Two prospective studies, two countries, the same direction.

And a third is underway: **PRISM**, the first large randomized trial of screening AI in the United States — a **$16M PCORI-funded, 7-site** study using Transpara, announced **September 2025** and recruiting now. Results are years away, but for the first time the US single-reader workflow will be tested directly.

*Citations: MASAI — Gommers/Lång et al., Lancet 2026;407:505–514. PRAIM — Eisemann et al., Nature Medicine 2025.*

## Slide 3 — 2026: the averages look strong — so where do these models still fail?

So it is 2026 — where do we actually stand? The averages look strong, but a single AUC says nothing about where a model fails. In our audit of a commercial DBT model across **163,000 exams** from EMBED, the **overall AUC was 0.91** — but look at how the scores break down by imaging feature. Masses, asymmetries, and calcifications are correctly predicted negative in **60–81% of exams**. But architectural distortions behave very differently: even screen-detected cancers split into a bimodal high-and-low distribution, and many benign distortions score high — so the model both misses cancers and over-flags benign findings in that one category. Performance depends on what the finding looks like, not just the headline AUC. The two gaps that remain: subgroup performance, and explainability.

*On-slide:* Exam-level model-score distributions by imaging feature, across clinical outcome.

*Citation: Nat Commun 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-70637-3, Fig. 6.*

## Slide 4 — We are at the brink of Era III.

So detection works — we now have the prospective evidence. But here is what is striking: it is still 2026, and the very same mammogram — and the tissue that follows it — already tells us far more than whether there is a cancer today. From this one screening image we can read a woman's future risk and a cardiovascular signal; from her biopsy slide, a molecular recurrence score. We are standing at the brink of **Era III** — the shift from finding the lesion to characterizing the patient — and, remarkably, much of it is available right now.

*On-slide:* From one screening mammogram and its biopsy — a future-risk estimate, a cardiovascular signal, and a molecular recurrence score. The shift from finding the lesion to characterizing the patient.

## Slide 5 — For 40 years, the only risk signal we could read off the image was density.

For forty years, the only risk signal we could read off the image was breast density — coarse and subjective. The clinical models we layered on top, Gail and Tyrer-Cuzick, were weak: Tyrer-Cuzick predicts five-year risk at an **AUC around 0.62**, barely better than chance at the level of the individual.

*On-slide:* Gail · Tyrer-Cuzick · BCSC — questionnaire + density models. **Tyrer-Cuzick v8 · 5-yr AUC ~0.62. Gail model · 5-yr AUC ~0.55–0.58. Coin flip is 0.50** — for the woman in front of you, barely above it.

---

## Slide 6 — Image-based risk: research model → cleared product.

The image itself carries risk. Yala and colleagues at MIT and MGH showed a deep network reading the mammogram out-predicts the questionnaire models — **Mirai**, externally validated across **three countries** at a **C-index of 0.76–0.81**. Among women who developed cancer within five years, it flagged **41.5% as high-risk versus 22.9% for Tyrer-Cuzick**. And this is no longer just research: in June 2025 **Clairity** received **FDA De Novo authorization** — the first tool to predict five-year breast-cancer risk from a routine screening mammogram alone, and it was added to the **2026 NCCN** guidance. Research model to cleared product to guideline in under five years. There is not yet a primary peer-reviewed validation paper for Clairity.

*On-slide bar chart:* % of 5-yr cancers flagged high-risk — **Mirai 41.5% vs Tyrer-Cuzick 22.9%**.

*Citation: Yala et al., Sci Transl Med 2021 · Clairity FDA De Novo 2025; NCCN 2026 — no primary validation paper yet.*

## Slide 7 — AsymMirai: much of the signal is local bilateral asymmetry.

Here is explainability done right, and it is our group's work with Duke. We asked what Mirai was actually keying on. The answer was surprisingly simple: local bilateral dissimilarity — the difference between left and right breast tissue in the same region. A transparent model built only on that asymmetry nearly matched the black box: **one-year AUC 0.79 versus 0.84**, and in stable-tissue subgroups **three-year AUC 0.92**. A risk signal you can point to on the image, and audit for where it fails. Performance you can explain is performance you can audit.

*On-slide:* Architecture diagram — Mirai (black box) vs AsymMirai (interpretable), with the asymmetry module detail (CC asymmetry, MLO asymmetry → average risk score).

*Citation: Donnelly et al., "AsymMirai," Radiology 2024;310(3):e232780 (Duke + Emory, EMBED 210,067 mammograms) · DOI 10.1148/radiol.232780.*

## Slide 8 — The model reports BAC as a continuous burden (mm²).

Here is what the model does. A transformer segments the breast arterial calcification on the routine mammogram and reports its burden as an area, in square millimeters — the original image on the left, the model's segmentation on the right. You can see it scale, from **mild calcification at about 5.6 mm²**, to **moderate at ≈18 mm²**, to **severe at over 100 mm²**. That continuous number is what turns an incidental finding into a graded, quantitative risk marker. Our group built the segmentation model behind this, SCU-Net.

*On-slide:* Mammogram (left) and model segmentation (right) at increasing BAC burden.

*Citations: Dapamede et al., Eur Heart J 2026;47(18):2206–2220 (Emory + Mayo) · segmentation: Guo et al. (SCU-Net), Med Phys 2021.*

---

## Slide 9 — Dose-dependent, and incremental to PREVENT.

And here is the result. Across **123,762 women** with no known heart disease — the largest study of its kind — that burden shows a continuous, dose-dependent association with major adverse cardiac events and all-cause mortality. These are the Kaplan–Meier curves for any MACE, stratified by BAC severity: a clean graded separation, **log-rank p < 0.0001**. Adjusted for the AHA PREVENT score, versus zero BAC: **mild HR 1.32, moderate HR 1.75, and severe HR 3.29 internally / 2.80 in external validation**. **Each additional mm² adds 2–3% of MACE risk (p < .001).** Crucially, this is incremental to PREVENT — it adds information the standard score does not capture.

*On-slide:* Event-free survival (any MACE) by BAC severity; combined Emory + Mayo cohort.

*Citation: Dapamede et al., Eur Heart J 2026;47(18):2206–2220 · Kaplan–Meier, any MACE, combined cohort.*

## Slide 10 — Estimating the Oncotype DX score from the H&E slide.

And this is the capability imaging simply does not have — the third model from the opening. Oncotype DX, the 21-gene recurrence score, **costs about $4,000 and takes 1–2 weeks**. **Orpheus**, trained on **6,172 cases across three institutions**, infers that Recurrence Score directly from the H&E slide — and it not only flags high-risk disease better than a leading nomogram, it predicts recurrence even among low-score patients, beating the score itself: **AUC 0.89 vs 0.73** for flagging high-risk (RS > 25), and **0.75 vs 0.49** for predicting recurrence in low-RS patients. And because it is built on segmented tissue, you can see what it keys on — tumor cells, lymphocytes, stroma. Morphology to molecular identity, read straight off the glass.

*On-slide:* Cell-segmentation micrographs — tumor cells, lymphocytes, stroma (Fig. 4 e–h).

*Citation: Boehm et al. ("Orpheus"), Nat Commun 2025;16:2106 · concept: Kather et al., Nat Cancer 2020.*

## Slide 11 — And we are on the verge of multimodal, multi-omics.

Step back and notice what every one of these has in common. Risk from the image, a cardiovascular signal from the calcium, a molecular score from the tissue — each is still one model reading one modality. The real frontier is to stop doing that: to combine the image, the pathology, the genome, and the clinical record into a single model of the patient. We are right at the verge of that — multimodal, multi-omics AI. And to see where it goes, we have to leave 2026.

*On-slide:* Everything so far is still one model, one modality. The frontier is to combine them — imaging + pathology + genomics + the clinical record — into a single model of the patient.
