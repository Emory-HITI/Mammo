Adoption

One model for everything is the wrong goal.

We chased better detection for three decades. But look at what we ask a single model to do at once:

01Hold up across every scanner and vendor in the field
02Perform in women of all ages, all densities, all risk types
03Serve both the expert breast radiologist and the generalist who reads mammo one day a week
04Fire at an operating point everyone agrees on — when no two readers agree how many false positives are worth one more cancer

A single model that satisfies all of these is not a hard engineering problem. It is a malformed one.

Adoption

Four walls — and the reframe.

1Generalization. The model that excels at one site degrades at the next. Across 275,900 mammograms (4 vendors, 7 sites, 2 countries), generalization evidence was still lacking — fairness in one population can cost performance in another.
2Operating point. No single right threshold. Sensitivity vs. false positives is a value judgment, not a setting — the expert and the once-a-week reader don't want the same one.
3Predictions in a vacuum. The image-only model lacks the chart, so its misses look foolish and trust erodes. Adoption tracks interpretability, not just AUC.
4The integration tax. A perfect model dies if it can't reach PACS. One-off integrations don't scale; onboarding happens through a platform you already run.

The reframeThe future isn't the model that works everywhere — it's a portfolio: locally validated, locally tuned, multimodal (image + clinical + risk), delivered inside existing workflow. The winning system is the one the radiologist trusts, that knows what they know, and that fires inside PACS without an IT ticket.
