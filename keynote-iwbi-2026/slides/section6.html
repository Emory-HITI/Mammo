Gap 1: the aggregate number hides the failures.

Breast (DBT), Nat Commun 2026: overall AUC 0.91, but in-situ 0.85 (sens 0.55), calcifications 0.80 (0.66), dense breasts 0.88 (0.63).

Neuro (ICH), npj Digit Med 2025: overall sensitivity 82.2%, but subacute 45.5%, chronic 54.8%, outpatient 72.2%.

Both were demographically robust; the blind spots were clinical — the subtle cases where help is needed most. Subgroup-stratified performance should be a release criterion.

Gap 2: we usually cannot say why.

When a model misses an in-situ cancer, we mostly cannot explain it, so we cannot anticipate the next miss. AsymMirai showed models can be built to be interrogated — a transparent signal you can point to on the image. Most deployed tools are not; they are black boxes.

And these models learn signals we never intended and cannot see — keying on scanner, site, and acquisition artifacts that have nothing to do with the cancer. Without explanation, subgroup auditing is the only check — and most deployments skip it.

Brittle data, drift we don't monitor, humans we de-skill.

Transfer: a model trained at one set of sites degrades at the next — new scanners, protocols, case mix. The fix begins with data: large, multi-site, representative training and validation — yet frameworks still don't require developers to disclose dataset composition.

Drift: FDA authorized ~1,250 AI/ML devices by early 2025 (~1,450 by mid-year, ~76% radiology) and finalized PCCP guidance (Dec 2024) — yet few carry one. A clearance is a snapshot, not a guarantee.

De-skilling: the human-in-the-loop can be the weak link. Oversight is a design problem, not a checkbox.

Europe writes the rules the world inherits.

EU AI Act — in force 1 Aug 2024. Medical AI is largely high-risk: risk management, data governance, transparency, human oversight, robustness, post-market monitoring. High-risk obligations apply from Aug 2026; AI medical devices under MDR transition by Aug 2027.
