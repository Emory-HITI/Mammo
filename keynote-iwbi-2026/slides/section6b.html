SpecialistvsGeneralist·an open question

Will the generalists absorb the field?

Every model in this talk was purpose-built. The frontier models from OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic were not built for medicine — yet they improve every few months. Do we still build our own?

Frontier models

Where the generalists already perform well.

Medical knowledge: frontier models score ~90%+ on USMLE-style exams (Med-Gemini 91.1%), across the breadth of medicine. Rapid adaptation: fine-tuned on small labeled sets they often exceed bespoke models, and generalize better across sites, scanners, and populations.

USMLE-style: ~91% (Med-Gemini)
2D CXR reports "equivalent or better" in 43–96% of cases
exceeded prior SOTA on 17/20 CXR tasks
Med-Gemini — Yang et al., arXiv:2405.03162, 2024 (preprint)
Frontier models

Where they still fall short — the image itself.

Their strength is text, not image content. On radiology interpretation, general VLM diagnostic accuracy ranges only ~8–29%, and roughly 22% of generated reports contain factual errors.

Purpose-built RadFM outperforms GPT-4V on radiology tasks. And 3D lags: Med-Gemini-3D CT reports were only 53% clinically acceptable — fine for a 2D film, less so for a volume.

Visual LLMs in Radiology, Life (MDPI) 2026;16(1):66 (PMID 41598221) · RadFM — Wu et al., Nat Commun 2025 · Med-Gemini, 2024
Text knowledge ≠ reading the image
Frontier models

Specializing a model does not make it safer.

On a hallucination benchmark (Med-HALT, 2025), general-purpose models were hallucination-free 76.6% of the time, versus only 51.3% for medically "specialized" models. Narrowing a model can erode its calibration.

general models: 76.6% hallucination-free
"specialized" models: 51.3%

The specialist-versus-generalist question is unsettled — and the instinct to buy the medical one may be wrong.

Med-HALT (MIT Media Lab medical-hallucination eval, 2025; arXiv 2307.15343)
Frontier models

The thesis: the "bitter lesson."

Over decades, general methods that scale with data and compute have repeatedly outperformed systems built on handcrafted domain knowledge — in chess, in vision, in language.

Radiology AI has spent fifteen years handcrafting. The trajectory of other fields suggests the generalists eventually take over perception too. The open question is when — and what we build in the meantime.

Rich Sutton, "The Bitter Lesson," 2019 essay (an argument, not a study)
Frontier models

A likely answer: orchestration.

The probable future is a stack, not a replacement. A frontier model as the reasoning and orchestration layer — it reads the EHR, talks to the patient, writes the report, decides the next step — calling specialist models as instruments: the detector, the risk model, the BAC quantifier, the tissue model.

generalist = reasoning & orchestration
specialists = the instruments it calls
increasingly domain-adapted (Med-Gemini)

Measurement still rewards purpose-built precision and calibration. "Agentic radiology": the generalist conducts; the specialists play.
