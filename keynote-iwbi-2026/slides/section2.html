Era II

2016: the first large-scale push — and a surge of optimism.

The Digital Mammography DREAM Challenge was the first large-scale effort to build breast-cancer AI — and we took part. The hope was that a crowdsourced competition would simply solve it.

1,000+ participants · 126 teams · 44 countries
~640,000 mammograms

Best method AUC 0.858 → ensemble 0.895 → ensemble + a radiologist 0.942. The winner, Therapixel, spun out MammoScreen — still in clinical use today.

Schaffter et al., JAMA Network Open 2020;3(3):e200265.

Era II

A single AUC hides where a model fails.

Our DBT audit (EMBED, 163,449 exams): overall AUC 0.91 — but sensitivity falls on the subtle, high-stakes cases (dense, in-situ, calcifications).

It doesn't transfer: "Beyond Screening" — models trained on screening dropped on diagnostic exams and fell to specificity 31–54% on women with implants.

The two gaps: subgroup performance + explainability.

Trivedi group — Nat Commun 2026 (DBT) · Du H et al. "Beyond Screening" 2026. Corroborated: RSNA 2023 challenge, BreastScreen Norway.

AUC stays high; sensitivity collapses on the hard cases
Era II → Era III

The detection score rises for years before the cancer is visible.

Hickman et al., Radiology 2026 — three detection systems across sequential screens (31,394 individuals). For future cancers the AI score is elevated up to 10 years before diagnosis; cancer-free individuals stay flat.

Combined predictive AUC 0.63–0.67 vs 0.57 for breast density.

Detection is blurring into risk. We stop asking the image about the lesion — and start asking it about the patient. That is Era III.
