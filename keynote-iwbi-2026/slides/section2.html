The reframe
Modern screening AI decouples workload from accuracy.

Read more, miss less, recall no more often. Where double-reading is standard and readers are short — most of Europe — that is a different operating model, not a marginal gain.

Caveat emptor

A single AUC hides where a model fails.

Gap 1 — subgroup performance. A commercial DBT model: overall AUC 0.91, but lower on in-situ cancers, calcifications, and dense breasts — the subtle, high-stakes cases. The same pattern held auditing an ICH model (82% sensitivity overall → 45% subacute). Both were demographically robust; the blind spots were clinical.

Gap 2 — explainability. When the model misses, we usually cannot say why — so we cannot anticipate the next miss. Subgroup auditing is the only safety net, and most deployments skip it.

What we still don't know
State the limits plainly.
›No mortality data yet. Endpoints are detection, recall, workload, and interval cancer — a strong surrogate, not mortality.
›Generalizability. Pivotal RCTs are Swedish/European, mostly single-vendor, in double-reading programs. The US uses a single reader.
›The honest driver is operational — workforce capacity — as much as diagnostic superiority. That is a good reason, not a weak one.

So that is the lesion: today's cancer, finally found with evidence. The same mammogram holds more than today's cancer — so we stop asking about the lesion and start asking about the whole image.
