Era II

2016: the first large-scale push — and a surge of optimism.

The Digital Mammography DREAM Challenge was the first large-scale effort to build breast-cancer AI — and we took part. The hope was that a crowdsourced competition would simply solve the problem.

1,000+ participants · 126 teams · 44 countries
~640,000 mammograms
winner (Therapixel) AUC ~0.87
AI + one radiologist beat either alone

A company spun out of it — Therapixel's MammoScreen — and it is still in clinical use today.

Schaffter et al., JAMA Network Open 2020;3(3):e200265 (DREAM Challenge ran 2016–17)
Era II

2020: an AI that beat the average radiologist on paper.

McKinney et al., Nature 2020 (Google Health, UK + US). A deep-learning system read screening mammograms stand-alone.

false positives −5.7% US / −1.2% UK
false negatives −9.4% US / −2.7% UK
AUC +11.5% vs average radiologist
second-reader workload −88%
McKinney SM et al., Nature 2020;577:89–94
Era II

Then the field checked itself.

Haibe-Kains et al., Nature 2020 noted that the McKinney result withheld code and model details, so it could not be independently reproduced.

This is the difference from CAD, which was adopted without that scrutiny. Here, the concern was raised from inside the field — before deployment, not after.

Haibe-Kains B et al., Nature 2020;586:E14–E16
Era II

Independent groups confirmed it on external data.

Salim et al., JAMA Oncology 2020 (Stockholm, 8,805 women). The best algorithm reached AUC 0.956; AI plus a first reader reached 88.6% sensitivity at 93.0% specificity — exceeding two human readers.

Salim M et al., JAMA Oncol 2020 · external, multi-vendor
Era II

Promising — but retrospective is not the bar.

Enriched case sets, lab conditions, no real workflow. CAD had good retrospective numbers too. The bar is prospective deployment in a screening program.

So what do you do in 2020? You wait for prospective evidence.

The same trial: AI triage let one radiologist do what took two — against a real, growing reader shortage. More cancers, far less reading.

Lång et al., Lancet Oncology 2023 (44.3% workload reduction; false positives flat)
Era II

The endpoint that matters: interval cancers did not rise.

The worry with reading less is missing cancers that surface between screens. MASAI's primary endpoint settled it.

interval cancer 1.55 vs 1.76 /1,000 — non-inferior (ratio 0.88)
sensitivity 80.5% vs 73.8% (p=0.031)
specificity 98.5% in both

The first RCT to show AI-supported screening does not raise the interval-cancer rate — level-1 evidence, the bar CAD never cleared.

Gommers et al. (…Lång), Lancet 2026;407(10527):505–514 · PMID 41620232

Era II

A single AUC hides where a model fails.

Our DBT audit (EMBED, 163,449 exams): overall AUC 0.91, but lower on the subtle, high-stakes cases — in-situ 0.85, calcifications 0.80, dense breasts 0.88 (sensitivity far lower still).

It doesn't transfer. "Beyond Screening": trained on screening (AUC 0.78–0.81), models dropped on diagnostic exams and fell to AUC 0.69–0.76 / specificity 31–54% on women with implants.

Robust across demographics; the gaps were clinical. The two gaps: subgroup performance + explainability.

Trivedi group — Nat Commun 2026 (DBT); npj Digit Med 2025 (ICH); Du H et al. "Beyond Screening" 2026. Corroborated: RSNA 2023 challenge, BreastScreen Norway, ARIES.

Headline 0.91 → 0.80 on calcifications
Era II

A few limitations, stated plainly.

01
No mortality data yet. Detection, workload, and now interval cancer (a strong surrogate) — but not breast-cancer deaths prevented. Don't overclaim.
02
Generalizability. The pivotal RCTs are European, single-vendor, double-reading. The US screens with a single reader, so the workflow gains may not transfer. MASAI did not collect race/ethnicity.
03
Reproducibility. Many models still lack open code; retrospective AUCs often decline under independent validation.
04
In-situ / overdiagnosis is not fully settled (MASAI in-situ ratio ~1.51).
05
The main driver is operational — workforce capacity — as much as diagnostic superiority. A legitimate reason, worth stating directly.
Era II

The detection score rises for years before the cancer is visible.

Hickman et al., Radiology 2026 — three detection systems across sequential screens (31,394 individuals). For future cancers the AI score is elevated up to 10 years before diagnosis; cancer-free individuals stay flat.

Proportion flagged at the 90th centile: ~19% at 6 yr → 24% at 4 yr → ~38% at 2 yr. Combined AUC 0.63–0.67 vs 0.57 for density.

A score trained to detect current cancer also carries information about future cancer.
