Hidden signals in the image.

A normal mammogram. Two readers, both right — no cancer today. But a model reads the same pixels and forecasts her five-year risk. The question changes: from "is there cancer now?" to "what is her risk, and how hard should we look?"

The mammogram stops being only a detector. It becomes a biosensor — and this one is already cleared by the FDA.

Era III · the pivot

The pixels carry risk — beyond density, beyond the questionnaire.

Yala et al. (Radiology 2019) showed a network reading the image itself out-predicted the classical models. Mirai (Yala et al., Science Translational Medicine 2021) was built to predict across time points, tolerate missing risk-factor data, and stay consistent across scanners.

C-index 0.76 / 0.81 / 0.79 — validated US · Sweden · Taiwan
41.5% of future cancers flagged high-risk (vs 22.9% Tyrer-Cuzick)

But a number like 0.81 is a black box answer. For a screening program, "it works" is not enough — we have to know why.

Yala et al., Radiology 2019 · Mirai — Yala et al., Sci Transl Med 2021 (DOI 10.1126/scitranslmed.aba4373)
Era III · explainability done right
Why does it work? The signal is local bilateral asymmetry — and you can see it.

Mirai is accurate but opaque. AsymMirai (Donnelly et al., Radiology 2024 — Duke + Emory) reverse-engineered what it keys on: the left-vs-right tissue difference. A transparent model on that signal alone nearly matched the black box.

Era III · the signal builds early
The asymmetry intensified years before the cancer.

One patient, left MLO, same region across three screening rounds. The model's attention localizes to a growing asymmetry the readers reasonably called normal — and that is where the cancer arose.

Risk is a trajectory, not a snapshot.The score was rising for years before the diagnosis.
Same phenomenon as detection.Hickman (§2): detection scores also climb across a decade before we call it cancer.
Era III · the trap in a good AUC

A population AUC is not an individual prediction.

Discrimination ≠ calibration. An AUC measures how well a model ranks a million women. Whether a predicted "8% risk" actually means 8% — calibration — is a separate property, less often reported, and it is the one that governs her decision.

~28% of future cancers in the AI top decile
~21% in the clinical-model top decile
the two high-risk groups overlap only partially

The image model and the clinical model flag different women. That disagreement — not the average AUC — is the decision in front of you.

Arasu et al., Radiology 2023;307(5):e222733

Era III · already cleared, already in trials

From research model to cleared product to guideline — in under five years.

Clairity Breast (FDA De Novo, June 2025) — the first tool to predict 5-year risk from a routine screening mammogram alone; added to the 2026 NCCN screening guidance.

WISDOM (JAMA 2026): risk-based non-inferior for stage ≥IIB · ~3,800 fewer mammograms /100k person-yr · 89% chose risk-based
MyPeBS: ~85,000 women · 6 countries · screen-by-risk vs by-age · ongoing

Today the trials triage on genetics and classical models. The image-derived score is the second axis they have not yet switched on.

Clairity — FDA De Novo 2025 (no primary validation paper yet) · WISDOM — Esserman et al., JAMA 2026;335(9):763–774 · MyPeBS — BMC Cancer 2022
