What thirty years taught us.

Don't bet on the architecture — bet on the discipline. Across three decades, the arc runs from promise, to practice, to prevention.

Promise, practice, prevention.

Promise
CAD. We marked the spot and scaled it before we proved it. It failed.
Practice
Detection, proven. Whole-image AI earned randomized evidence — and the same image now reads risk, the heart, the molecular score. It works.
Prevention
The destination. Risk known before she enters the scanner — the mammogram as a whole-body health visit.

The discipline, in three lines.

01Prove it before you scale it. Prospective, randomized evidence — MASAI is the bar, not a retrospective AUC.
02Audit the blind spots. Explainability and subgroup performance are release criteria — a headline AUC is a sales number.
03Protect the human. Design against automation bias and de-skilling — the clinician should stay a clinician, not a rubber stamp.
Close

The image hasn't changed. What we can read from it has.

Twenty-five years ago we could barely mark a suspicious spot. From that same image today: a woman's cancer risk years ahead, her cardiovascular risk, and a link to her tissue and genome.

Close

The science is here. The task is to deliver it.

From a single mammogram we can now read a woman's five-year cancer risk, her cardiovascular signal, and her tumor's molecular biology — alongside detection that earned randomized evidence.

The remaining work is to deploy it with the discipline this field lacked the first time: prove it, audit it, keep the clinician in the loop.
