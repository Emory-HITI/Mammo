# Thirty Years, Three Eras: AI in Breast Imaging from CAD to Clinical Intelligence
### Keynote — IWBI 2026 (International Workshop on Breast Imaging), Greece
> **Official title:** *Thirty Years, Three Eras: AI in Breast Imaging from CAD to Clinical Intelligence.* The "three eras" (CAD → deep-learning detection → clinical intelligence) are the macro-frame; **lesion → image → patient is the within-talk spine. The title card previews the three eras.
**Speaker:** Hari Trivedi, MD (Emory University) · **Duration:** 30 minutes · **Audience:** physicians + scientists (broad, international)

---

## Thesis

AI in breast cancer reflects a widening question of what we ask the image to tell us: from the lesion, to the image, to the patient. Traditional CAD aimed to help us see the lesion and did not improve outcomes. 

Modern deep learning finds lesions on the image better than CAD but still has gaps in explainability, subgroup performance, and generizability across populations. 

Future-facing models can use the whole image and even multi-modal data (multimodal radiology or pathology) to gain even more information about the patient and their prognosis.

How will these models change our practice as radiologists? How does the role of a breast radiologist look different 10 years from now. And will frontier models from trillion dollars companies make radiologists obsolete? 

**Through-line spine (state it in the hook, repeat at every transition):**

> ### LESION → IMAGE → PATIENT

- **Lesion** — find the spot, draw the circle. CAD → modern detection/screening AI. *(Sections 1–2)*
- **Image** — the whole image predicts future cancer and cardiovascular risk beyond the lesion. The mammogram as biosensor. *(Section 3)*
- **Patient** — combine imaging, tissue, genome, and history into one representation. Personalized screening using MR and MRI. Pathology and clinical intelligence. *(Sections 4–5)*

## The five compelling themes (the spine the audience should remember)

1. **Reimbursement before evidence, mistake of CAD.** CAD was reimbursed before it was validated and did not improve outcomes. Modern AI obtained randomized-trial evidence (MASAI) before scaling. The lesson is the method, not the machine alone.
2. Deep learning is better, still has gaps in explainability and subgroup performance. Ground truthing is now the hardest part of model development, noisy data is not the ceiling of model performance.
3.Clinical Intelligence
  a. **From detection to risk prediction.** AI shifts a screening image from "is there cancer today?" to "what is this person's risk, and how closely should we look?"  (Mirai → FDA-cleared Clairity).
  b. **Multimodal combinations of imaging and pathology.** Pathology AI infers genomics, receptor status, and recurrence risk from an H&E slide, a capability imaging lacks and a bridge to integrated decision-making.
  c. Cardiovascular risk prediction using BAC and whole images
4. Governance and deskilling is a big challenge
